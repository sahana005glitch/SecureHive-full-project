Federated identity lets a user authenticate with one organization's credentials to access resources belonging to a different, trusted organization — common in partner and vendor access scenarios.

## Key points

- Trust relationships in federated identity are typically established through signed metadata exchanged between the identity provider and service provider.
- This model is common in higher education (eduGAIN) and B2B SaaS integrations where a customer's employees need access without a separate account.
- Misconfigured trust relationships are a common source of federated identity vulnerabilities, since a compromised partner IdP can grant unauthorized access.

## The takeaway

Federated identity extends the SSO convenience across organizational boundaries, but it also extends the trust boundary — and the risk — accordingly.
