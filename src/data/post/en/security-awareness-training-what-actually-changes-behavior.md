Annual, generic security training has a well-documented poor track record — behavior change requires a fundamentally different approach.

## Key points

- Frequent, short training sessions outperform annual all-day sessions for actual retention and behavior change, according to most security awareness research.
- Simulated phishing tests combined with immediate, non-punitive feedback teach recognition far more effectively than lecture-style training alone.
- Making security reporting genuinely easy (a one-click button) and positively reinforced increases reporting rates more than punishing mistakes does.

## The takeaway

Security awareness training works when it's treated as an ongoing behavior-change program, not an annual compliance checkbox.
