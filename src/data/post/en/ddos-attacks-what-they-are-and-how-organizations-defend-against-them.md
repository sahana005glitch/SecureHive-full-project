A distributed denial-of-service attack floods a target with traffic from many sources at once, aiming to make a service unavailable rather than to steal data.

## Key points

- Modern DDoS attacks can combine volumetric floods with application-layer attacks that mimic legitimate user traffic, making filtering harder.
- Content delivery networks and dedicated DDoS mitigation services absorb and filter malicious traffic before it reaches origin servers.
- Some DDoS attacks are used as a smokescreen to distract security teams while a separate, quieter intrusion happens elsewhere.

## The takeaway

DDoS defense is now table stakes for any public-facing service, and mitigation should be planned before an attack, not during one.
