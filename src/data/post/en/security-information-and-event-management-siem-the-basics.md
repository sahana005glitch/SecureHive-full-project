A SIEM aggregates log data from across an organization's systems into a central platform, enabling correlation, alerting, and long-term retention for compliance.

## Key points

- Effective SIEM deployment depends heavily on proper log source coverage — a SIEM can't detect what it never receives data about.
- Alert tuning is an ongoing process; a poorly tuned SIEM generates so many false positives that real alerts get missed in the noise.
- Many compliance frameworks (PCI DSS, HIPAA) effectively require SIEM-equivalent logging and retention capabilities to pass audit.

## The takeaway

A SIEM is only as useful as the log sources feeding it and the tuning applied to its alerting — deployment is the easy part; operationalizing it well is the hard part.
