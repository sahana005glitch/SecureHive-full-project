Cyber insurance has become a standard part of enterprise risk management, but coverage details and exclusions vary significantly between policies.

## Key points

- Many policies now require specific security controls (MFA, EDR deployment) as a condition of coverage, effectively using insurance requirements to drive security improvement.
- Coverage for nation-state attacks is a frequent point of dispute, with some insurers invoking 'act of war' exclusions for certain high-profile incidents.
- Incident response costs, business interruption, and regulatory fines are commonly covered, but ransom payment coverage varies and is sometimes explicitly excluded.

## The takeaway

Cyber insurance is a risk transfer tool, not a substitute for security controls — and increasingly, insurers require the controls anyway before offering coverage.
