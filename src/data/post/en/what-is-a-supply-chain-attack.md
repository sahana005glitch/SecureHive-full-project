A supply chain attack compromises a trusted vendor, software update, or component to reach the actual target indirectly — often affecting hundreds of organizations through a single compromised source.

## Key points

- The SolarWinds incident remains the reference case: attackers compromised a software update mechanism trusted by thousands of organizations.
- Open-source software dependencies represent a growing supply chain risk, since a single compromised package can propagate to every project that depends on it.
- Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) practices are increasingly required by regulation specifically to give organizations visibility into their software supply chain.

## The takeaway

Supply chain attacks are effective precisely because they exploit trust relationships that traditional perimeter security doesn't examine.
