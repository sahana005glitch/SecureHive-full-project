EDR tools monitor endpoint activity continuously, looking for behavioral indicators of compromise rather than relying solely on known malware signatures.

## Key points

- Traditional antivirus catches known threats by signature; EDR is designed to catch novel or fileless attacks by observing suspicious behavior patterns.
- EDR tools typically maintain a detailed activity timeline, letting incident responders reconstruct exactly what happened during a compromise.
- Many EDR platforms include automated response capabilities, like isolating a compromised endpoint from the network the moment malicious behavior is detected.

## The takeaway

EDR shifts the detection model from 'do we recognize this file' to 'does this behavior look wrong' — a necessary evolution as malware increasingly avoids detectable signatures.
