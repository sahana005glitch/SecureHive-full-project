Sufficiently powerful quantum computers could theoretically break the public-key cryptography that secures most of today's internet traffic — though practical, large-scale quantum computers don't yet exist.

## Key points

- 'Harvest now, decrypt later' describes the strategy of storing encrypted data today with the expectation of decrypting it once quantum computing matures.
- NIST has already standardized several post-quantum cryptographic algorithms designed to resist attacks from future quantum computers.
- Organizations handling long-lived sensitive data (decades-long confidentiality requirements) are the ones with the most urgent reason to begin migration planning now.

## The takeaway

Post-quantum migration is a multi-year undertaking, which is exactly why security teams are being encouraged to start planning well before the threat becomes practical.
