Brute force attacks systematically try many password combinations until one works, relying on weak or predictable passwords rather than any specific vulnerability.

## Key points

- Account lockout policies and rate limiting are the most direct defenses, slowing attackers down to the point of impracticality.
- Attackers increasingly use 'low and slow' brute force — spreading attempts over days or weeks to stay under detection thresholds.
- Enforcing minimum password length (12+ characters) does more to resist brute force than complexity rules alone.

## The takeaway

Brute force attacks are simple in concept but still effective against systems without proper rate limiting or lockout controls.
