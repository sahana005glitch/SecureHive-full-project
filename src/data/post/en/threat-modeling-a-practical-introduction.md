Threat modeling asks a structured set of questions early in system design — what are we building, what could go wrong, and what are we doing about it — before code is even written.

## Key points

- The STRIDE framework (Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information disclosure, Denial of service, Elevation of privilege) is one common structured approach to threat modeling.
- Threat modeling is most valuable during design, when changes are cheap, rather than after implementation when fixing architectural issues is far more expensive.
- Simple threat modeling — even a whiteboard session with the right questions — provides most of the value; it doesn't require expensive specialized tooling to start.

## The takeaway

Threat modeling shifts security left in the development process, catching design flaws before they become expensive-to-fix vulnerabilities.
