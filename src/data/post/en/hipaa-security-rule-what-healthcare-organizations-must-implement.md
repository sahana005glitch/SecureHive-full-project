The HIPAA Security Rule sets specific administrative, physical, and technical safeguard requirements for protecting electronic protected health information (ePHI).

## Key points

- Risk analysis is explicitly required and must be documented, updated regularly, and used to inform actual security decisions — not just filed away.
- Business Associate Agreements are required with any third party that handles ePHI on a covered entity's behalf, extending compliance obligations down the vendor chain.
- Encryption is technically an 'addressable' rather than strictly 'required' safeguard under HIPAA, but its absence must be specifically justified and compensating controls documented.

## The takeaway

HIPAA compliance failures are disproportionately about lacking documentation and process, not just missing technical controls.
