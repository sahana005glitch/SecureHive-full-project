Business email compromise doesn't rely on malware at all — it relies on convincing someone to act on a fraudulent request that looks like it came from a trusted colleague or executive.

## Key points

- Attackers often compromise or spoof a real executive's email account, then time the fraudulent wire request around travel or end-of-quarter urgency.
- BEC losses regularly exceed losses from ransomware in FBI reporting, precisely because there's no malware for antivirus tools to catch.
- The single most effective control is a verification step outside of email — a phone call to a known number — before any wire transfer or credential change.

## The takeaway

BEC succeeds by exploiting trust and urgency, not technical vulnerabilities, which is exactly why technical controls alone can't fully stop it.
