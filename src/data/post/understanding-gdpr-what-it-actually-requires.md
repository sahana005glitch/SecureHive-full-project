GDPR reshaped how organizations worldwide handle personal data of EU residents, with fines that can reach a meaningful percentage of global annual revenue.

## Key points

- GDPR applies based on whose data is processed (EU residents), not where the processing organization is headquartered — a common point of confusion.
- The regulation requires breach notification within 72 hours of becoming aware of a qualifying breach, a tight timeline that requires pre-established incident response processes.
- Data subject rights — including the right to access, correct, and delete personal data — require organizations to actually be able to locate all instances of an individual's data.

## The takeaway

GDPR compliance is as much an operational and data-mapping challenge as a legal one — you can't protect or delete data you don't know you have.
