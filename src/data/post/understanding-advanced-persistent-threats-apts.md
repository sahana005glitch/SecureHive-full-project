An advanced persistent threat describes a long-term, targeted intrusion — typically by a well-resourced actor — designed to maintain access and avoid detection for extended periods.

## Key points

- APT groups often spend months in reconnaissance before taking any action that could be detected, studying the target's specific defenses.
- Persistence mechanisms like scheduled tasks, backdoored accounts, and compromised update mechanisms let APT actors maintain access even after initial detection.
- Threat intelligence sharing between organizations and government agencies has become a key tool for identifying APT infrastructure and tactics.

## The takeaway

APTs represent a different threat model than opportunistic attacks — defense requires assuming a sufficiently motivated attacker will eventually get in, and focusing on detection and containment.
