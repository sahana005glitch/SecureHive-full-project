Least privilege — granting only the minimum access necessary for a task — is one of the oldest security principles and still one of the most consistently under-implemented.

## Key points

- Privilege creep, where users accumulate access over time as roles change without old access being revoked, is the most common way least privilege erodes in practice.
- Regular access reviews and time-bound access grants are the two most effective controls against privilege creep.
- Least privilege applies equally to service accounts and API integrations, which are frequently over-provisioned during initial setup for convenience.

## The takeaway

Least privilege is simple to state and genuinely difficult to maintain over time — which is exactly why it requires ongoing process, not a one-time implementation.
