A strong security culture means employees consider security implications naturally, not because a policy document told them to — and that requires leadership modeling, not just documentation.

## Key points

- Security teams that are approachable and helpful (rather than seen as the department that says no) get reported issues faster and more often.
- Visible executive participation in security initiatives — like leadership completing the same phishing training as everyone else — signals genuine priority.
- Blameless post-incident reviews encourage people to report mistakes and near-misses instead of hiding them out of fear.

## The takeaway

Policy documents describe the rules; culture determines whether people actually follow them when no one's watching.
