Red team exercises go beyond penetration testing's technical scope to simulate a full adversary campaign — including social engineering and physical security testing.

## Key points

- Unlike a scoped penetration test, a red team engagement typically operates with minimal advance warning to the organization's own defenders (the blue team).
- Red team exercises test not just technical vulnerabilities but detection and response capability — does the security team even notice the simulated attack?
- Purple teaming — collaborative exercises between red and blue teams — has grown in popularity specifically to maximize the learning value of these exercises.

## The takeaway

Red teaming answers a different question than a penetration test: not just 'can we be breached' but 'would we even notice if we were'.
