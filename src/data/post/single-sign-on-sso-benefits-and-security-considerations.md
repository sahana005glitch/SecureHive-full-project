SSO lets users authenticate once to access multiple connected applications, reducing password fatigue — but it also concentrates risk into a single point of failure.

## Key points

- A compromised SSO account can grant an attacker access to every connected application at once, which is why SSO accounts warrant the strongest available MFA.
- SAML and OAuth/OIDC are the two dominant protocols underlying most enterprise SSO implementations, each with different security considerations.
- Conditional access policies (requiring device compliance or specific network locations) add an additional layer of control on top of SSO authentication.

## The takeaway

SSO is a net security improvement for most organizations, but it raises the stakes on protecting the identity provider itself.
