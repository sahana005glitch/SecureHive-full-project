Fingerprints, facial recognition, and other biometric factors offer convenient authentication — but they come with a risk profile fundamentally different from passwords.

## Key points

- Unlike a password, a compromised biometric can't be changed — your fingerprint is the same fingerprint for life.
- Modern implementations store a mathematical representation of the biometric, not the raw image, specifically to limit damage if the data is breached.
- Biometrics work best as one factor in a multi-factor scheme, not as a standalone replacement for all other authentication.

## The takeaway

Biometric authentication trades a different kind of risk for convenience — it's not inherently more or less secure than passwords, just differently vulnerable.
