Credential stuffing takes username/password pairs leaked in one breach and automatically tries them against dozens of other services, betting on password reuse.

## Key points

- Automated tools can test millions of stolen credential pairs per hour against login pages that don't rate-limit attempts.
- Because the attacker already has a valid password, these attacks often bypass basic anomaly detection that looks for guessing patterns.
- Rate limiting, CAPTCHA on repeated failures, and mandatory MFA are the three controls that blunt this attack most directly.

## The takeaway

As long as password reuse remains common, credential stuffing remains one of the highest-volume, lowest-effort attacks available.
